Why this exists

General assistants can read your voice note. They just can't always transcribe it.

A general assistant will happily identify a voice note — codec, container, duration, sample rate — and then tell you it has no speech recognition available in that session. Whether it transcribes is not something you can predict before you need it. This does one job, and tells you plainly when it cannot: every failure has a named cause and a way forward, because it depends on providers we do not control.

The formats voice notes actually arrive in

Chat voice messages are usually Opus in an Ogg container; forwarded between iPhone apps they arrive as AAC in .m4a. We take both, and detect the format from the file's content rather than its extension — because the extension is often wrong.

One complete job

Not a step in someone else's workflow. Drop in a recording and get everything back at once: the words, a translation if you need one, the debrief — and a visual if you want it.

Nothing to install, nothing stored

Everything runs in this browser. There is no backend of ours holding your audio, no account and nothing to install — though the audio itself is sent onward for transcription, which is the next section.

Where your audio goes

We cannot tell you who processes your recording

This used to describe three privacy modes and let you pick one. Two of the three did not exist, so it was not a choice — and offering a privacy selector implies a control we do not have. Here is what actually happens:

Your audio and its transcript are sent to OpenRouter, which passes them to a model provider. We do not know which provider handles your recording, and we have no control over what they do with it. Do not use this for anything you would not hand to a third party.

What we genuinely do not hold

There is no backend of ours. Your audio is never stored on a server we run, there is no account, and nothing about you is tracked. Your key stays in this browser's local storage and goes only to OpenRouter with each request.

What that does not protect you from

The recording still leaves your device, because transcription happens at a model provider. "No backend" is a statement about us; it is not a statement about them.

What would change it

Processing on your own device would mean nothing leaves at all, and restricting to named providers would mean we could tell you who. Neither is built. When one is, it will appear here as a thing you can use, not as a mode you can select and then not have.

A full privacy notice is being written and will be published here; until it is, this section and the open repository are the record.

Getting the audio

Six ways a recording reaches this page

Voice notes are the common case and chat apps are the common source — but nothing here is specific to one app. If it is audio, it works.

Chat voice messages

WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram. On a laptop: open the web or desktop client, hover the message, download. On Android: share or export the message. Arrives as .opus or .ogg.

iPhone Voice Memos

Share → Save to Files, or AirDrop it to yourself. Arrives as .m4a.

Android recorder

The built-in Recorder app, or whatever your phone ships. Share → Save, usually .m4a or .ogg.

Meeting recordings

Zoom, Meet and Teams all export an audio-only file. Use that rather than the video — it is smaller and costs less to transcribe.

Dictaphones and cameras

Anything that writes a file to a card. If it is .m4a, .ogg or .opus, drop it in.

Anything else

Format is detected from the file's content, not its name, so a wrongly-named file usually still works. If it genuinely cannot be read, you get told which format arrived.

What it costs

There is no plan, no credit and no card. You pay OpenRouter directly.

This page used to describe a credit product with prices. It does not exist, and labelling it PROPOSED did not stop it reading as a price list — so it is gone. What is true today: you bring your own OpenRouter key, every model call is billed to you by them, and we add no margin because there is nothing of ours in the path to add it to.

What a pass actually cost

Measured on real runs of a 90-second note: a few tenths of a penny for the transcript, the metadata and the debrief together. The infographic is the expensive part when you ask for one — it is an image model.

You see the ceiling first

Before anything runs, the page quotes the maximum this pass can cost with the options you chose. A run can come in under it — a recording already in your language does not pay to be translated — and it cannot go over without stopping.

We do not set the price

OpenRouter does, per model, and it changes. Any figure here is something we observed, not something we charge. Set a spend limit on your key and the ceiling is yours, not ours.
