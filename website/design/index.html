Family one

Three themes — the same flow, three ideas about how to guide you

These differ in look, and in how you are told what is happening and what comes next. None of them uses the current navy-and-green, deliberately: the test should measure the design, not which one looks familiar.

Family two

Four culture packs — not translation, adaptation

Two languages, four cultures. Each pack changes the accent colour, the typography, the shape language, the register and the vocabulary — plus money, dates and number formats. What we are asking

Tell us what you would actually rather use

Opinions on any of it are welcome, but these are the questions the test exists to answer:

On the themes

  • At the moment you land — is it obvious what to do first?
  • While it runs — do you feel told what is happening, or left waiting?
  • Before you spend anything — was the cost clear, and did you trust it?
  • Which of the three would you send to a friend?

On the cultures

  • If one is your culture: does it read as written for you, or translated at you?
  • Anything that feels off — a word, a tone, a colour that means something else where you are?
  • Is the UK and US split real enough to be worth doing?
  • Portugal and Brazil: right call to treat them as separate, or overkill?

Honest notes. These are design prototypes, not the shipping product — the live app is at /app/ and is unaffected by anything here. They run on a small runtime that exists only under /design/; the product itself has no framework. The transcript and summary in demo mode are a fixed sample, and the demo is labelled as costing nothing because it calls nothing. These pages are excluded from search indexing and from the sitemap.

The reasoning behind both families is public: the design briefs and hand-off packs, and the review that accepted them.
