title: The pass reads the note before deciding what to do with it — and the Updates can read themselves aloud
---

- The pass now **classifies before it translates** (issue 061): a step between transcribing and summarising that reports the detected language, topics, register, mood, urgency and safety signals, shown in a new card above the transcript
- It pays for itself by deciding whether translation is needed at all — an English note for an English reader now spends about **$0.0015 against a $0.11 quote** instead of paying to translate itself into the language it is already in
- The quote on screen stays honest while that happens: the price you are shown assumes every optional branch is taken, and the run then declines the work it discovers it does not need. **A pass may spend less than quoted, never more**
- The safety signals include prompt injection, credentials spoken aloud, and the request-for-money-plus-time-pressure pairing that is the shape of most voice-note fraud — labelled **observations, not a security check**, because the classifier reads the same untrusted words as everything else and can be talked out of reporting
- Nothing the classifier returns is ever put back into a prompt, and every value it produces has to match a known list: a voice note must not be able to write our instructions
- The Updates page can **read itself aloud** (issue 062): a 🎙 side pane that lists the published posts, pre-fills an editable news-style script, offers six voices, and hands you the `.wav` — your own OpenRouter key, made in your browser, nothing uploaded and nothing stored
- It reports what a read **actually cost** by looking the generation up afterwards, rather than repeating "a fraction of a penny"
- A brief for publishing those readings properly — a spoken script written into each post, an enclosure in the feed so it becomes a podcast, a player on the page — grounded against the real text-to-speech module rather than its documentation, which got two things wrong. Written, not built
- Internal: the workflow state machine gains a `facts` namespace with two invariants enforced by the runner rather than by convention — a discovered fact may never *add* a step, and a step that degrades can still declare what it learned
