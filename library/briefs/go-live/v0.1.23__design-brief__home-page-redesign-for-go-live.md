# Design brief — home page redesign for go-live: **VoiceDebrief for WhatsApp**

**To:** Claude Design (or any designer picking this up)
**From:** Dinis Cruz, via the Conductor
**Date:** 8 August 2026 · repo version `v0.1.23`
**Status:** brief — nothing here exists yet

---

## The ask, in one paragraph

We are **going live**, under a new name, and the home page has to be rebuilt for that.
Three things change at once. The product is now **VoiceDebrief for WhatsApp** at
**VoiceDebrief.ai**. The **main workflow moves onto the home page** — a visitor should
be able to drop in a voice note and get a debrief without navigating anywhere. And the
site has to become **honest about what it actually is today**: a beta, running on
somebody else's models, with no privacy guarantees we are in a position to make. That
last part is not a disclaimer to bury; on this product it is a design problem worth
solving well, because the people who care about it are the people we most want.

---

## What the product actually is, today

Read this as the source of truth. Everything else on the current site is either true,
out of date, or aspirational, and part of your job is to tell us which is which.

Someone exports a voice message from WhatsApp — usually 30 seconds to 3 minutes, one
person talking to another — drops it on a page, and gets back:

| Artefact | What it is |
|---|---|
| **transcript** | What was said, in the language it was said. Never overwritten. |
| **translation** | The transcript in the reader's language. Optional, on by default. Its own card, not a replacement. |
| **debrief** (currently labelled "summary") | Key points, actions with an owner, and one sentence. Built from the translation when there is one. |
| **infographic** | One drawn image of the debrief. Only if asked. |

It runs **entirely in the browser**. There is no backend, no account, and nothing of
the user's is stored on any server of ours. The user brings their own **OpenRouter API
key**, which stays in their browser; every model call is billed to them directly. A
whole pass costs them a fraction of a penny and the maximum is quoted on screen before
they commit.

Four cultures ship today: `en-GB` (reviewed), `en-US`, `pt-PT`, `pt-BR` (translated but
not yet reviewed). Each has a real URL — `/app/pt-pt/` — so a translated page can be
linked directly.

## The five requirements

### 1. The workflow belongs on the home page

Today the home page sells the product and a **Try the beta →** button sends you to
`/app/`. That is one click too many for a tool whose entire proposition is *drop it in
and you are done*.

Design the home page so **the drop zone is the page**. A visitor should understand what
this is and be able to use it without navigating. How much explanation survives above,
below or around the workflow is your call — but the thing that makes this product good
is that it has no setup, and the current home page hides that behind a button.

One real constraint: **the user needs an OpenRouter key before anything can run**.
That is the actual first-run experience and it is currently a wall. Designing the path
from *"I just landed here"* to *"I have a key and my first debrief"* is the most
valuable thing in this brief. See requirement 4.

### 2. Delete the privacy modes; replace them with the truth

The app currently shows three privacy chips — **Routed — cheapest** / **Restricted*** /
**On-device*** — of which only the first does anything. The other two are marked with
an asterisk and disabled.

**Remove all three.** Shipping a privacy *selector* where two of three options do not
exist reads as a claim we cannot support, and it invites exactly the wrong assumption:
that a privacy control exists, so privacy is handled.

In their place, say plainly what happens, in the first person, where a user will see it
before they upload rather than after:

> Your audio and its transcript are sent to **OpenRouter**, which routes them to a model
> provider. **We do not know which provider handles your recording, and we have no
> control over what they do with it.** Do not use this for anything you would not be
> comfortable handing to a third party.

That is the substance. **Write it better than we have** — but do not soften it into
nothing, and do not let it read as boilerplate. Some ideas worth testing: place it at
the drop zone rather than in a footer; make it as visually confident as the rest of the
page rather than grey and small; treat "we can't promise which provider" as a statement
of integrity rather than an apology.

Do not remove the honesty in order to make the page convert better. If a design decision
trades that away, we would rather have the worse conversion.

### 3. A **beta** mark, and an LLM caveat

**Beta**, in the Gmail sense — a small persistent mark that lives beside the product
name, not a banner that shouts. It should be visible on every page, and it should read
as *"this is early and we are telling you"* rather than as an excuse.

And the caveat every LLM product needs, phrased for a person rather than a lawyer:
**this runs on large language models, which can get things wrong and can invent things
that were never said.** That matters more here than in most products, because a
transcript *looks* authoritative. Worth thinking about where this belongs — next to the
transcript itself, once results appear, may be more honest than a line on the home page
nobody reads.

### 4. A page explaining how to get an OpenRouter key

New page. Right now a first-time visitor hits *"bring your own OpenRouter key"* and has
no idea what that means. The page should get somebody from nothing to a working key:
what OpenRouter is, why we use it instead of billing them ourselves, how to sign up,
where the key is created, what it costs in practice (fractions of a penny per voice
note), how to set a spend limit, and how to revoke it.

Design the page, and design **where it is reached from** — the moment somebody needs it
is the moment they see the key field, not the moment they browse a menu.

### 5. Rename to VoiceDebrief for WhatsApp

The official name is **VoiceDebrief for WhatsApp**; the domain will be
**VoiceDebrief.ai**. The current name — *Voice Note Transcribe* — is hardcoded in **43
places** across the site and in all four locale files.

Two things we want from you rather than from us:

- **The wordmark.** How the name sits in the header, how "for WhatsApp" is treated
  (subordinate? a separate line? dropped in some contexts?), and how it works beside
  the beta mark.
- **Whether "summary" should become "debrief" in the interface.** The product is now
  named after that word. Our concept scheme records `summary` as a concept whose label
  could reasonably change — this is the moment to decide, and it affects four languages.

A naming caution we have already hit: **WhatsApp is Meta's trademark.** We can use it
descriptively ("for WhatsApp") as competitors do, but it cannot lead the product name
and we should not imitate WhatsApp's own branding. Our green is close enough to theirs
to be worth a second look.

## Also worth fixing, since you are in here

- **The current home page copy oversells.** It says "an analysis" as a distinct output;
  there is no analysis step — there is a debrief. Please go through the page and tell us
  every claim that is not exactly true today. We would rather cut a good line than keep a
  false one, and going live is when that stops being a stylistic preference.
- **The ASCII pipeline diagram** on the current home page is doing real work — people
  understand the product from it. Whatever replaces it should carry the same information:
  one pass, four artefacts, two of them optional.
- **Pricing.** There is a Pricing page describing a credit product that does not exist;
  today the only model is bring-your-own-key. Either it becomes an honest "what this
  costs you on your own key" page, or it goes.

## Constraints that are not negotiable

These are architectural, not stylistic. A design that violates one cannot be built here.

1. **No backend.** Static hosting. No server-side rendering, no database, no sessions,
   no server-side analytics. Anything stateful is `localStorage` in the user's browser.
2. **No SPA frameworks.** Static HTML, vanilla JS and Web Components with Shadow DOM.
   No React, no build step for the front end. Components are immutable and versioned.
3. **All colour comes from CSS custom properties.** The entire design is one stylesheet
   of `--wa-*` tokens on `:root`, which every component reads through the shadow
   boundary. **Design in tokens** — if your design needs a new colour, it is a new token,
   and CI checks that every token read is declared.
4. **A strict Content Security Policy.** No CDN fonts, no external scripts, no third-party
   embeds, no trackers. Everything self-hosted. A design that needs a web font must ship
   it as a file we can host.
5. **Four cultures, and text that grows.** Portuguese runs 15–25% longer than English.
   Any layout that only works at English string lengths will break; the language picker
   lives top-right in the nav and must stay reachable.
6. **Mobile is not secondary.** People receive voice notes on their phone, and that is
   where they will try this. We have already shipped one dropdown that opened off the
   left edge of an iPhone. Design phone-first for the workflow.
7. **Accessibility is not optional.** Keyboard reachable, real focus states, sufficient
   contrast on the navy, and never colour as the only carrier of meaning.

## What we would like back

1. **The home page**, desktop and phone, with the workflow on it — including the empty
   state, the no-key state, the working state and the results state. The no-key state is
   the one that decides whether this product succeeds.
2. **The honesty surfaces**: how the OpenRouter routing statement, the beta mark and the
   LLM caveat appear, and where. This is the part we most want a designer's judgement on,
   because we will get it wrong in the direction of either burying it or being tedious.
3. **The OpenRouter key guide page.**
4. **The wordmark** and a recommendation on `summary` vs `debrief`.
5. **A list of every claim on the current site that is not true**, which you are better
   placed to notice than we are.

Markdown, HTML, images, or a prototype — whatever carries it. If you produce HTML, it
does not need to be production-grade; we will rebuild it in our own architecture, as we
did with the previous design handoff.

## Honest tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Workflow on the home page vs explaining the product | The tool's virtue is having no setup, and the home page currently hides that behind a button — but a stranger still needs to know what this is before they hand it a recording |
| Honesty vs conversion | The routing statement will cost us some users. That is the correct trade and we do not want it optimised away |
| A key before anything works | Bring-your-own-key is the whole beta. It is also a wall at the exact moment someone is most willing to try |
| "Beta" as reassurance vs excuse | Gmail's beta signalled confidence. Ours must not read as "so do not expect much" |
| Renaming during go-live | 43 hardcoded strings and four locale files. Doing it late is worse; doing it badly is worse still |
| Green | Ours is close to WhatsApp's, which helps recognition and risks implying endorsement by a company whose mark we may only use descriptively |
