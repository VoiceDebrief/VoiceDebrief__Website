# 00 — Read me first: the ask

**version** v0.1.23 · **date** 8 August 2026 · **type** briefing pack, entry point

---

## In one paragraph

VoiceDebrief turns a WhatsApp voice note into a transcript, a translation, a short
written debrief and optionally an infographic — all in the browser, with no backend.
Two briefs (documents 03 and 04 in this pack) propose a layer above that: pull a
**concept structure** out of each recording, and map subsequent recordings against a
scheme the customer already has, so fifty voice notes become one searchable index in
the customer's own vocabulary. **We have built the concept model and a review surface
for it. We have built none of the extraction or mapping.** Before building any of it,
we want to see what the data actually looks like — because the visualisation cannot be
designed from a schema, only from real material.

## What we want from you

**Produce a markdown simulation of semantic graphs over a realistic corpus of voice
notes**, from a single fictional organisation, over time — so that accumulation,
drift and failure-to-map are all visible in worked examples rather than argued about
in prose.

The full specification is in [document 06](v0.1.23__pack__06-the-ask-what-to-simulate.md).
The short version:

1. **Invent a corpus.** One fictional organisation, 8–12 WhatsApp voice notes recorded
   over a few weeks by two or three people. Realistic speech — hesitation, backtracking,
   half-finished sentences, names, numbers, dates. Not clean prose.
2. **Run the real pipeline on paper.** For each note, write the transcript, then the
   summary **in the exact shape our summary prompt produces** (`data/summary-prompt.md`
   — three fixed sections). The graph work must consume what the product actually
   emits, not an idealised input.
3. **Extract, then map.** Free-extract a concept scheme from note 1. From note 2
   onward, map against what already exists and record three outcomes: what mapped,
   what mapped approximately, and **what did not map at all** — which is the output
   the briefs care most about.
4. **Show the failure modes**, deliberately. Two notes naming the same idea differently.
   A concept that should have been split. A term that only makes sense in one speaker's
   vocabulary. These are the cases the visualisation has to make legible.
5. **Show the accumulated scheme** after the whole corpus, and say what changed about
   it along the way.
6. **Then propose the views.** Given that data, what should a person actually see?
   Sketch them in markdown/ASCII/mermaid. This is the part that becomes our
   implementation spec.

## What would make this useless

- **Inventing a new data shape.** Use the schema in `data/concepts.json`. Extend it
  where you must, and say explicitly what you added and why.
- **Starting from the transcript's ideal.** Voice notes are messy. A simulation over
  clean, well-structured speech will produce a graph that is easy to draw and wrong.
- **Assuming a backend.** No server, no database, no vector store. See document 01.
- **Ignoring cost.** Every model call is money on the user's own key, and the whole
  pass is budgeted at $0.30. A design needing 100 calls per recording is not a design.
- **Producing only a pretty graph.** The most valuable output in both briefs is the
  thing that *failed* to map. If your simulation makes that hard to see, it has
  reproduced the problem rather than solved it.

## What we will do with it

The markdown you produce comes back into this repository as the specification for:

- the shape of an extraction/mapping step in the declared workflow,
- the operations the chat surface emits to edit a scheme,
- and, most importantly, **the visualisation** — which we will build against your
  worked examples rather than against a guess.

## One warning about the briefs in this pack

Documents 03 and 04 are Dinis's, reproduced verbatim, and they are good. Document 05
is our engineering review of them, and it identifies **three places where building
them literally would go wrong** — most importantly a measured finding about batch size
that the brief applies to the wrong step, in a way that manufactures a cost problem
that need not exist. **Read 05 before designing anything.**
