# 05 — Engineering review of both briefs

**version** v0.1.23 · **date** 8 August 2026 · **from** Claude Code (this repository)

---

Both briefs are good and their central theses hold. This document records where
building them **literally** would go wrong, grounded in the code rather than in
argument. Anyone designing from documents 03 and 04 should read this first.

## Three corrections

### 1. Windowing is not sweeping — and the difference dissolves the cost problem

Brief 04 takes the measured finding that *"presenting five concepts at a time
consistently outperformed ten"* and concludes:

> the mapping step should window the scheme into small slices rather than presenting it
> whole, which also keeps the cost per recording bounded as a customer's ontology grows

and then lists the consequence in its own tensions table as *"many calls per recording
as an ontology grows, which is a cost curve to watch."*

**That cost curve is an artefact of the misreading.** Five-at-a-time is the size of the
**adjudication batch over retrieved candidates** — it is not a sweep of the scheme.
Step 2 of the brief's own pipeline exists precisely so that the whole scheme is never
presented. With retrieval in place:

- cost is **O(unmapped spans)**, not O(concepts) — roughly one to three model calls per
  recording, **flat** as the ontology grows past 500 concepts;
- without it, a 500-concept ontology costs 100 calls per recording.

That second figure is not hypothetical against our numbers. The whole pass is budgeted
at **$0.30**, with transcribe at $0.05 and summary at $0.02
(`data/standard-workflow.json`). A mapping step fits the existing quote line only under
the retrieval reading. Under the sweep reading it **cannot be a workflow variant at
all**, because its cost cannot be quoted before it runs.

**Design consequence for the simulation:** show the retrieval step explicitly, and show
how many candidates a real span actually produces. If the answer is "usually 2–3, never
more than 8", the whole matcher gets simpler.

### 2. Embedding retrieval is not available in this architecture

There is no backend, and we have only ever called chat completions through OpenRouter.
Whether OpenRouter usefully exposes an embeddings endpoint is **unverified — we have
never called one**. A vector store is out of the question: nothing runs server-side.

This probably does not matter, because at our scale **lexical retrieval in the browser
is enough**. Normalised token overlap (or BM25-lite) over a few hundred concepts is
instant, free, and adds no dependency. And the brief's own finding that *definitions
improve matching* helps **more** here than it would with embeddings: a definition and
scope note give a lexical matcher far more surface to match against than a bare label.

So the pipeline we would build is the brief's with one substitution:

```
   1. EXACT MATCH        normalised label + altLabel comparison
   2. LEXICAL TOP-5      over label + altLabel + definition + scopeNote,
                         in the browser, free, instant
   3. THE MODEL          adjudicates the 5, given definitions not labels
   4. FILTERS            cardinality and consistency rules
   5. THE PERSON         the unresolved, and everything that mapped to nothing
```

**Design consequence for the simulation:** when you show a mapping decision, show the
*lexical* candidates it came from — including the near-misses that scored well but were
wrong. Those are what tell us whether step 2 can really be lexical.

### 3. Format symmetry: agree at the core, split the file

Brief 04 says the interface vocabulary and a customer ontology *"are the same kind of
object, and one visualisation, one editor and one storage format cover both."*

The **core** transfers exactly: `id`, `definition`, `scopeNote`, `broader`, `narrower`,
`related`, labels. But the shipped `concepts.json` also carries **review-only** fields
(`verdict`, `finding`, `proposed`, `current`, `keys`) that mean nothing for a customer
ontology — and a customer ontology needs fields it lacks, above all **provenance**:
which recording produced this concept, when, at what confidence, and by whose decision.

"The same model" is right. "The same format" will be read as "the same file", and then
either the interface vocabulary grows provenance fields or the customer ontology grows
verdicts. State it as **one core schema, two profiles**.

**Design consequence for the simulation:** propose the provenance fields concretely.
That is the part we cannot design without seeing the data.

## Two things the briefs under-weight

### Our input unit is a voice note, not a document

Every piece of research cited in brief 04 indexes documents — legal instruments,
learning content — yielding dozens of concepts each. **A 60-second voice note yields
maybe 3 to 8.**

So the open question *"how does a customer without a scheme start?"* has a harder answer
than the brief implies: bootstrapping a usable scheme probably needs **20 to 50
recordings, not three**. That is a real time-to-value problem for the valuable half, and
it is the strongest argument for building extraction first despite the brief's own
warning — not because it demos well, but because it is the only route to a scheme worth
mapping against.

**This is the single most important thing for the simulation to test.** Build the corpus
large enough that the answer is visible: at what point does the scheme stop changing
shape on every new recording?

### The sticky asset is also the sensitive one

Brief 04 argues for persistence from **retention**: an ontology refined over fifty
recordings is what a customer would be unwilling to lose.

It should argue it from **risk** in the same breath. An ontology distilled from fifty of
someone's private voice notes is a *more* revealing artefact than any single transcript
— it is the structure of what they talk about, who with, and about what. Under this
repository's ciphertext rule that is not a footnote; it decides whether this can live in
`localStorage` at all. Note also that the virtual file system and commit queue brief 04
points at live in a **different repository** (SG/Send) and are not wired here.

## Where the briefs are right, and already half-built

**"Chat must emit operations, not replacement schemes."** This pattern is already
shipping. `chat-tools.js` exposes `update_transcript`, `update_summary` and
`restore_original`, tiered `changes materials`, and the page renders *"✎ edited by the
assistant · restore the original"*. Operation, provenance, undo — working today.
Ontology operations should be **new tools in that same registry**, using the same tier
vocabulary, not a new mechanism.

**"What fails to map is the finding."** Already a house pattern here, three times over:
CI records screenshot changes rather than blocking on them; the privacy line states what
it cannot promise; the concept scheme's `decide` verdict marks what could not be
resolved rather than guessing. The simulation should treat the unmapped report as a
first-class artefact with its own layout, not as an error list.

## One small architectural gap worth closing early

`pipeline.js` contains:

```js
export const WORKFLOW_URL = './workflows/standard.json'
```

A single hardcoded constant. Themes and locales both already have `index.json`
allowlists under this repo's rule that **selection names a key, never a URL**. A
`workflows/index.json` completes the set and is roughly twenty lines. It should exist
before there is a second workflow, not after — and "ontology mapping is a workflow
variant" means there will be a second workflow.

## Summary of what we would change

| Brief says | We would build |
|---|---|
| Window the scheme five at a time | Retrieve top-5 candidates, adjudicate those. Cost flat in ontology size |
| Embedding retrieval | Lexical retrieval over label + altLabel + definition + scopeNote, in-browser |
| One format for both schemes | One core schema, two profiles — customer ontology adds provenance |
| Local storage now, vault later | Same, but the risk argument makes the vault more urgent than the retention argument does |
| Mapping is a paid workflow variant | Agreed — needs `workflows/index.json` first |
| Chat emits operations | Agreed — as tools in the existing tiered registry, reusing the edit/restore pattern |
