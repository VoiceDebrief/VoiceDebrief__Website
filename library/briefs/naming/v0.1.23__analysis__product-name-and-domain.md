## The one constraint that shapes everything: the WhatsApp mark

Every candidate on the list starts with "WhatsApp", so this comes first.
**"WhatsApp" is Meta's trademark, and Meta's brand guidelines prohibit using it
in product names, logos and domains in ways that imply the product is theirs.**
What IS defensible is *nominative* use — describing compatibility: "for WhatsApp
voice notes", "transcribe WhatsApp audio". Note that this is exactly what the
competitors in the SERP do: **TalkNotes** ("Transcriber *for* Whatsapp") and
**Zapia** brand themselves, and put WhatsApp in the description. Neither is
called "WhatsApp anything".

So the honest frame is: **the product name and logo cannot lead with WhatsApp;
the copy, titles and metadata absolutely should.** The name carries the brand;
the strings carry the SEO.

## The SERP reality (the screenshots)

The head query — "whatsapp voice (memo|note) transcript(ion)" — is owned by
**WhatsApp itself**: since Nov 2024 the platform has built-in voice message
transcripts, so the Help Center, the official blog and Reddit threads about the
built-in feature take the top of the page. Below them sit the tool aggregators
(TalkNotes, Zapia) fighting for the remainder.

Two conclusions:

1. **"Transcription" is the commodity word — and it now names a free platform
   feature.** A product named "…Transcription" competes with WhatsApp for
   WhatsApp's own feature name, and even at its best wins a user whose need the
   built-in feature may already satisfy.
2. **Our winnable queries are about the OUTPUT, not the input**: the built-in
   feature produces a wall of text; nobody's built-in feature produces a
   structured summary, an analysis, a debrief, an infographic. Queries like
   "whatsapp voice note summary", "summarise whatsapp audio", "whatsapp voice
   note too long" have intent the platform doesn't serve — and the pt-PT/pt-BR
   locales (i18n strategy, issue 050) open the same long tail in the world's
   heaviest voice-note cultures.

| **WhatsApp Voice Transcript** | same | same | same | ✗ |
| **WhatsApp Voice Notes** | worst of all — it is literally the feature's own name | competes with every "how do I use voice notes" query | zero | ✗ |
| **WhatsApp Voice Memos** | bad — and "Voice Memos" is Apple's app name: two ecosystems' marks in one name | "memo" phrasing exists (your own search used it!) but is the smaller variant | zero | ✗ |

All five fail the same two tests: they brand us with someone else's mark, and
they describe the commodity half of the product (input + transcription) rather
than the half only we do (analysis + debrief, priced and declared up front).

## Recommendation: name the OUTPUT, describe the input

**Proposed name: `VoiceBrief`** — with the descriptive long form always beside
it:

> **VoiceBrief** — WhatsApp voice notes, turned into a transcript, an analysis
> and a debrief. In one pass.

Why it works:

- **It names the differentiator.** Competitors are transcribers; we deliver the
  *brief*. "Brief" also echoes this team's own culture (briefs, debriefs, the
  brief pack) — the product does to a voice note what the team does to a
  session.
- **Trademark-clean.** No Meta mark, no Apple mark. WhatsApp appears where it
  belongs: in the tagline, `<title>`, H1, descriptions and the subdomain —
  nominative, like the competitors.
- **Logo-able.** One glyph: a speech bubble folding into a document (bubble →
  brief). Keeps the established green dot / dark navy heritage; reads at
  favicon size. (This becomes an input to the Claude Design brief — the three
  A/B variants, issue 052, can carry the identity.)
- **Speakable and typeable** — two syllables, no hyphens, survives being said
  aloud in a video.

**Runner-up: `Voice Note Debrief`** — fully descriptive, zero brand risk,
weaker as a brand (three words, no compression), but a safe fallback if
VoiceBrief collides with something in a later domain/handle search. The current
"Voice Note Transcribe" is the same shape but names the commodity; if the name
stays descriptive it should at least name the debrief.

*(Also considered and set aside: "NoteBrief" — loses the voice; "Debrief" alone
— ungoogleable, likely collisions; "One Pass" — abstract, describes mechanism
not value.)*

## Domain

- **Now: keep `whatsapp-voice-transcription.sgraph.ai`.** It is indexed, it is
  the exact-match descriptive phrase, every canonical tag on the estate points
  at it, and as a *descriptive subdomain* it sits in nominative-use territory.
  A rename today re-earns indexing for zero gain.
- **`whatsapp.sgraph.ai`: recommend against.** Shorter, but it *strengthens*
  the implied-affiliation problem (a bare "whatsapp." host looks official),
  and it throws away the descriptive keywords that are the subdomain's whole
  SEO value. Worst of both.
- **Later, the dedicated domain should NOT contain "whatsapp"** (Meta's
  guidelines extend to domains) — a VoiceBrief-shaped domain with the WhatsApp
  keywords in titles/content is the durable posture. Do the availability
  search when the name is decided; this doc deliberately makes no claims about
  what is free.

## How we out-position the SERP (whatever the name)

1. **Own the output queries** the platform can't serve: summary/debrief/
   analysis phrasing, in every locale we ship (the i18n strategy's pt pair
   lands directly here).
2. **Say the price**: "well under £0.01, maximum shown before each run" is a
   sentence no competitor in that SERP says — put it in meta descriptions.
3. **Privacy as a claim with receipts**: "no backend — nothing is uploaded to
   us" out-positions bot-based competitors (TalkNotes routes your audio
   through their bot) and links to /engineering/security/ for proof.
4. **Agent discoverability**: `/llms.txt` + the machine-readable surfaces mean
   AI assistants can recommend and even operate the tool — a channel the
   SERP-fighters don't play in at all.
5. **The videos** (shorts on exactly these queries) give the "Short videos"
   SERP row a route in that doesn't require outranking WhatsApp's Help Center.
