# Public Site And Story Review

**date** 6 August 2026
**from** Claude (review session agent)
**type** Review — live site (fetched 6 Aug 2026)

## 1. Through Three Readers' Eyes

**The potential customer** gets a strong headline ("one pass"), one clear CTA, and an
app that works without signup — the sample chips lower the barrier to zero. But the
landing page never says "OpenRouter" (the hero badge "BRING YOUR OWN KEY" goes
unexplained until the app), the pricing section describes a credit/metered-key product
that is PROPOSED without saying so, and there is **no privacy policy, terms, about,
FAQ or contact anywhere**. A cautious user asked to paste an API key into a page with
no legal pages may simply bounce.

**The fellow GenAI entrepreneur** is the best-served audience — the honest privacy
copy, per-version diffs, and the "chat wrote the next issue's brief" stories are
catnip. Weakness: the actual thesis (*no backend, OpenRouter carries billing, the
margin is the key's credit limit, an agentic team builds it in the open*) is never
stated plainly on the site; the interesting bit requires digging through GitHub.

**The joining collaborator/agent** finds the Library page mapping almost 1:1 to the
session-start checklist — genuinely good onboarding. Friction: every content link
exits to GitHub blobs; there's no on-site "start here as a contributor" path.

## 2. "The Journey Is The Story" — Scorecard

**Working**: the Updates ("story") / Versions ("ledger") / Changelog ("complete
record") triad is coherent and self-described; per-tag diff links; QA-stamping
explained to readers; failures written up publicly. This is real build-in-the-open,
not garnish.

**Falling flat** (all verified against the live site):

1. **The ledger is stale**: footer says v0.1.20, `versions.json` stops at v0.1.18 —
   the flagship "every version" page is missing the two newest tags, including the
   chat work the newest post celebrates.
2. **Dead link on the newest post**: "issue 034 — the full write-up" →
   `blob/qa/issues/open/034...` → 404 (the issue moved to done and merged). Same
   fragility class in the other `blob/qa` link.
3. **Moving-target diffs**: posts link `compare/v0.1.13...dev` and
   `compare/v0.1.18...qa` — these change over time, undermining the permanent-record
   promise. Pin to tags.
4. **Landing copy overstates**: step 2 says "Pick your privacy mode" (the selector
   doesn't exist; the app page honestly disables the chips); "a full privacy notice
   ships with the beta" — the beta is live and no notice exists. The pricing section
   breaks the repo's own PROPOSED-labelling rule on its most commercially sensitive
   surface, and its "opens with the August 2026 beta" future tense now clashes with
   the "LIVE NOW" hero badge.
5. **The story doesn't travel**: zero OG/Twitter meta tags anywhere — social shares
   of the journey render as bare links — and no RSS/Atom feed on Updates. Real costs
   for a build-in-public strategy.
6. **No engineering surface**: CI status, test counts, the issue queue, the QA
   estate — all invisible on the site (see doc 06, the proposal this motivates).
7. Sharp-eyed readers will notice **`main` serves nothing**: the production domain is
   a dev build; the "two branches, two estates" public story doesn't match wiring.

## 3. Prioritised Site Suggestions

1. **Fix the record** (hours, highest trust impact): versions.json → v0.1.20;
   changelog headings; repoint the 404'd and `blob/qa` issue links to
   `blob/dev/issues/done/...`; pin the two moving compares to tags. Then add outbound
   link-checking of Updates/Library to the live-QA job — the harness already exists.
2. **Label pricing honestly**: split into "Today (beta): BYOK, ~£0.003–20p per pass,
   here's how to get a key" vs "Planned: £5 credits as your own capped metered key —
   PROPOSED". Converts a credibility liability into a build-in-open asset and
   resolves the LIVE-NOW/August-tense clash.
3. **Ship the minimum trust set**: a short privacy page (the no-backend architecture
   makes it easy and differentiating — "here's why we *can't* store your audio", with
   source links), about/contact, and a 5-question FAQ (key setup, cost, formats,
   phones, what OpenRouter sees). The privacy page doubles as the DPO's overdue
   notice (doc 04 §4).
4. **State the thesis on the landing page**: one section — no backend, OpenRouter
   carries billing, 11-role agentic team, everything published — with links inward.
   Add OG/Twitter tags and an RSS feed so the story travels.
5. **Build the engineering hub** (doc 06) — the single biggest missing piece of the
   strategy.
6. Later: render key docs natively (client-side markdown, in-pattern with the static
   stack) instead of sending every Library click to GitHub; reconcile the `main`
   story (doc 07, decision D4).
