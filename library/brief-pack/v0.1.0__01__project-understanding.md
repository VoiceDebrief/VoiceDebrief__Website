## What This Repo Is

`SGraph-AI__SaaS__WhatsApp__Audio__Transcription` is the shared product repository for the
**first SGraph product being taken to market**: a WhatsApp voice note transcription tool.
It is the productisation of tools Dinis built for himself and published on the SGraph tools
site (`SGraph-AI__Tools`), turned into a consumer product under the commercial arrangement
settled on 27 July 2026 (see `v0.1.0__03__commercial-model.md`).

## The Job

One job, done in one pass, in one smooth motion:

```
   drop in the audio (.opus / .ogg / .m4a)
            |
            v
   ONE PASS  ->  transcript
             ->  analysis
             ->  short debrief
             ->  infographic (only if asked)
            |
            v
   done. no accounts to configure, no steps to chain.
```

No accounts to configure, no steps to chain. This is the 23 July "complete jobs" principle:
"upload a thing and get a transcript" is a complete user-facing job. The product folds the
audio-transcribe tool and the infographic generator into one job, which is why it is a
better first product than either alone.

## Why It Exists — The Availability Gap, Not A Format Gap

The reason this product has a market was corrected by direct testing on 25 July 2026 and
the correction matters:

- WhatsApp voice notes are **Opus audio in an Ogg container** (the platform's format since
  2016, 48 kHz mono). Downloading from a laptop yields `.opus`. Forwarding between iPhone
  apps re-encodes to `.m4a` (AAC in MPEG-4) — a real but narrower path.
- A mainstream assistant, given a genuine `.opus` file, **parsed it perfectly** (correctly
  reporting codec, container, duration, sample rate) and still could not transcribe it:
  "no speech recognition engine available in that session."
- The barrier is therefore **capability availability, which the user cannot predict in
  advance** — not the file format. A format gap closes in one vendor release; unpredictable
  capability is a standing user problem, and the more durable thing to compete against.

Design consequences:
1. Accept both codec families (Opus/Ogg and AAC/MPEG-4), **detect by content, not extension**.
2. Modern browsers decode both natively — a front-end-only design is not fighting the format.
3. The pitch is reliability of one complete job, not "we support a format others don't".

## The Product Principles (Load-Bearing Decisions)

1. **No backend.** Everything runs in the front end; hosting is static (GitHub Pages, with
   S3+CloudFront where needed). Nothing server-side to secure, patch, scale, or pay for
   when idle. The only exception ever planned is a single key-provisioning Lambda — and
   even that is deferred (beta users get a capped hardcoded key).
2. **OpenRouter carries inference AND billing.** Per-user provisioning keys carry a credit
   limit; OpenRouter meters and deducts. The margin is implemented as the issued key's
   credit limit — configuration, not software. No billing system is built.
3. **Privacy is a mode, not a disclaimer.** Three selectable tiers:
   | Mode | Where it runs | Privacy | Price |
   |------|--------------|---------|-------|
   | routed (default) | any OpenRouter provider | no guarantee | cheapest |
   | restricted | chosen providers only | named processors | higher |
   | browser-local | the user's own device | nothing leaves | possibly free |
4. **One repo, three targets, two branches.** Web, iOS, Android from this one repository
   (Chrome extension deliberately later). CI mirrors `dev` → dev estate and `main` →
   production. The mobile signing/deployment path has been walked before for SGraph.
5. **Accounts are optional.** Google social login (already implemented elsewhere) or pure
   local-storage custody of the session + issued key, preserving the no-backend property.

## What This Does NOT Try To Be

- Not a backend service, not a billing system, not a private service by default.
- Not four targets at once (extension later), not a general transcription platform.
- One job. One pass. One awkward-to-serve audience served well.

## Honest Tensions To Hold

- Hardcoded beta key ⇒ must be hard-capped, treated as public, revocable, short-lived.
- The cheapest (default) mode is the least private — surface this honestly in the UI.
- All issued keys draw one pooled OpenRouter balance ⇒ the operator carries float.
- Speaker labels / timestamps / long files / languages are the obvious next requests, and
  each erodes the one-motion simplicity that is the product's advantage.
