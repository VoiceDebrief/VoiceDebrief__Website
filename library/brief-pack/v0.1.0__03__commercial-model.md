## The Arrangement (settled 27 July 2026)

- **Parties**: The Cyber Boardroom (Operator) and a partner entity, two legal companies,
  splitting **profit fifty-fifty** (not revenue).
- **Term**: ninety days from **1 August 2026** to 30 October 2026; lapses automatically
  unless extended in writing. Early exit on ~14 days notice, any reason.
- **The take**: locked at **25% of Net Receipts** (after payment-processor fees) — a
  one-third markup on cost of goods. The other **75% is provisioned onto the user's
  OpenRouter key** as their credit limit.
- **The split**: the retained 25% is divided evenly between Operator and Partner.
- **Costs**: the Operator carries them; they are minimal because the product is statically
  hosted with no backend. Neither party charges the other for time or for building its own
  tooling/agentic workflows.

## Billing By Not Building Billing

When a user buys credits (via Stripe), the Operator provisions an OpenRouter key whose
credit limit equals the user's Service Allocation (75% of net). OpenRouter meters and
deducts consumption. The commercial model is **a property of the key**, not a billing
system anyone has to build.

Working-capital reality: all issued keys draw on a single pooled OpenRouter balance, so the
Operator pre-funds consumption and holds the float. Small at these volumes, but a real
obligation, and user credits survive termination (the pool stays funded until consumed).

## The Fixed-Fee Floor

Because the processor fee has a fixed component (illustration: 1.5% + 20p), small top-ups
deliver poor value: a £1 purchase returns ~59p of inference to the user; £20 returns ~73%.
Therefore the **minimum credit purchase is £5.00**, with a pre-launch review of whether it
should be £10.00. Pricing targets **at or under 20p per transcription**.

Mobile note (parked): in-app purchase commission would exceed the entire take, so the
likely shape is web purchase with in-app consumption, subject to store rules.

## Openness Shrinks The Legal Surface

Everything produced is open source and published as it is produced, in this shared repo.
Neither party holds exclusive rights; either may fork and commercialise after the term.
The only confidential material: keys, passwords, financial data, personal data. With
nothing to allocate and nothing to keep secret, the contract collapses to the split, the
term, and a short list — eleven open decisions rather than eleven negotiated clauses.

The contract draft is explicitly a DRAFT by a non-lawyer, for review before signing.

## Success Criterion

The real output is a **reusable commercialisation kit** (token purchase, product page,
user handling). The test is whether the second product ships faster than the first.

## Open Commercial Points (from the draft)

- Minimum top-up: £5 or £10 (set by the fee structure, not intuition).
- The take basis for the browser-local mode (no cost of goods to sit against).
- Schedule A (the direct-cost list) — the most common source of later disagreement.
- Selling credits on mobile vs web purchase + in-app consumption.
- Licence choice for code (MIT or Apache 2.0 suggested; docs CC BY 4.0).
